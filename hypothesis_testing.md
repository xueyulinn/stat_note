# Hypothesis testing
A statistical procedure using sample data to decide whether there is
enough evidence to REJECT H0. It never proves a hypothesis true — the
only outcomes are "reject H0" or "fail to reject H0" (≠ accept H0) because there may be too many reasonable hypotheses.

# Null hypothesis (H0)
The default hypothesis, assumed to hold until there is strong evidence
against it. It's the one we try to reject, and it must be specific
enough to compute the distribution of the test statistic (e.g. μ = 500).

# Alternative hypothesis (H1)
The claim we turn to support when H0 is rejected (usually what the
researcher wants to show). Often the complement of H0, but not always
its strict logical opposite (e.g. one-sided or equivalence tests).
